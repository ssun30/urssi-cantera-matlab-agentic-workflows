Hi, I'm **Su Sun** — a postdoctoral scientific software engineer at Northeastern
University. I work at the intersection of reaction mechanism discovery, chemical
systems simulation, and scientific software engineering.

I maintain the **[Cantera](https://cantera.org) MATLAB interface**, and I care
about making powerful open-source scientific software usable — and *stay* usable —
from the tools that engineers and educators actually reach for.

## What I work on

- **Cantera MATLAB toolbox** — a modern, object-oriented MATLAB interface to
  Cantera's thermodynamics, transport, and chemical kinetics models,
  rebuilt through two major architectural overhauls and adopted by
  the community with 14k+ downloads.
- **Automated reaction mechanism generation** — contributions to the open-source
  [RMG-Py](https://reactionmechanismgenerator.github.io/RMG-Py/) and
  [ReactionMechanismSimulator (RMS)](https://reactionmechanismgenerator.github.io/ReactionMechanismSimulator.jl/latest/) ecosystems for electrochemistry and CVD
  applications, spanning Python and Julia.
- **Agent-assisted software workflows** — orchestrating multi-stage development
  processes (source-code generation, code-quality checks, unit testing,
  packaging, release, and distribution) for scientific computing, including work
  to reduce a custom-compiled interface to a single-click install and to cut
  runtime overhead substantially.

## Background

- **Ph.D., Chemical Engineering**, Northeastern University — 2025
- **M.S., Materials Science**, University of Southern California — 2017
- **B.S., Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering**, Georgia Institute of Technology — 2015

My doctoral dissertation, *Toward Automated Reaction Mechanism Generation for
Electrocatalytic CO₂ Conversion, Boron Nitride CVD, and Beyond*, focused on
automated reaction mechanism generation for electrocatalysis and thin-film
chemistry.
